Zesting & Juicing

The best flavour in a lemon is in its skin, and the most juice comes from a warm, well-rolled fruit. Here is how to get both.

1

Wash first

Scrub fruit in warm water before zesting to remove dirt and any wax. If you can, buy unwaxed fruit for zesting.

2

Zest before you juice

It is almost impossible to zest a squeezed lemon. Zest the whole fruit first, then cut and juice it.

3

Only the coloured layer

Zest is the thin, coloured outer skin. The white pith underneath is bitter. Use light pressure and turn the fruit as you go.

4

Warm and roll

Room-temperature fruit gives more juice. Roll it firmly on the board under your palm, or warm it in the microwave for 10–15 seconds.

5

Cut lengthwise

Cutting lemons and limes lengthwise, through the stem end, exposes more flesh and often releases more juice than a cut across the middle.

6

Strain the seeds

Squeeze over a sieve or through your fingers to catch the seeds. A simple citrus press doubles as a strainer.

7

Store it

Fresh juice keeps a few days in the fridge, covered. Freeze leftover juice in ice-cube trays and zest flat in a small bag.

8

Add at the end

Heat dulls citrus. Add juice and zest towards the end of cooking, or right before serving, for the freshest flavour.
