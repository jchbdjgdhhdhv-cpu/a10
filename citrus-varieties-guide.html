Citrus Varieties

Lemons and limes get most of the attention, but the citrus family is wide. Here is what each one tastes like and how to use it.

1

Lemon

Sour and fragrant

The kitchen workhorse. Juice for dressings and sauces, zest for baking and fish.

1 lemon ≈ 3 tbsp juice, 1 tbsp zest

2

Lime

Sharp and floral

Essential in Mexican, Thai and Indian cooking. More aromatic and a little more bitter than lemon.

1 lime ≈ 2 tbsp juice, 2 tsp zest

3

Orange

Sweet and mellow

Great in salads, glazes and baking. Navel oranges are seedless; Seville oranges are bitter, for marmalade.

1 orange ≈ 5 tbsp juice, 1.5 tbsp zest

4

Grapefruit

Bitter-sweet

Lovely segmented into salads with avocado or fennel. Pink varieties are sweeter.

1 grapefruit ≈ 10 tbsp juice

5

Mandarin

Sweet and easy

Includes clementines and satsumas. Easy to peel and perfect for snacking and desserts.

1 mandarin ≈ 2 tbsp juice

6

Blood orange

Berry-like

Deep red flesh with hints of raspberry. Beautiful in salads and sorbets during its short winter season.

1 blood orange ≈ 4 tbsp juice

7

Yuzu

Intensely aromatic

A Japanese citrus prized for its perfume. Mostly used as juice or zest, often in ponzu sauce.

Small and seedy: a little goes a long way

8

Kumquat

Eat it whole

Tiny fruit with sweet skin and sour flesh. Slice thinly into salads or candy whole.

Eat skin and all
